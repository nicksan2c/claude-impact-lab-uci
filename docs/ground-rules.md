# Ground rules

Affordable housing decisions change real families' lives. These rules keep your work honest and
keep people safe.

## Before you build

1. **Start with one real person** and the problem they're stuck on. A nonprofit finance lead, a
   permit tech, a congregation with a lot, a first-time buyer. Say who, and show what changes for
   them.
2. **Bend the brief if it serves the goal.** Teams may go outside the three lanes as long as the
   result helps get more affordable homes built in Southern California.
3. **Play fair with data.** Public or provided data only. Don't overload or abuse city websites.
   No private plans or blueprints without permission, and no personal information.

Live demos and sources are part of how judges score (see [judging](judging.md)): finalists must
demo live, and every law, dataset and assumption should be shown.

## More detail

### Personal information

**No personal information about real tenants, buyers or residents. From anyone. For any reason.**

- Do not bring, type, paste, photograph or upload information about a real person. That includes
  anything you know from your own job, even with the name removed.
- Test with made-up people. Say that they are made up.
- If you see something that looks like real personal information, stop and tell an organizer. Do
  not copy it, screenshot it or put it into any tool.

### Data

- Public or provided data only. The lane pages list starting points.
- Be kind to public servers. Download once and cache. Respect rate limits, `robots.txt` and each
  source's terms. If a city site starts failing, stop.
- Plans and blueprints that are not public need the owner's permission first.
- Parcel data is public. Owner names tied to people are personal information. Do not build a tool
  that profiles individuals.

### Prototypes only

- Nothing you build files an application, emails a city, or changes a real system.
- Draft the message or the form. Do not send or submit it.
- Your tool is not legal, financial or lending advice. Say so on screen.

### Do not invent facts

- No made-up statutes, section numbers, fees, deadlines, addresses or parcel facts.
- When the source does not say, your tool says it does not know.
- Laws get renumbered and income limits get revised. Show the date you checked.
- Numbers you assumed are labelled as assumptions, everywhere they appear.

### Secrets

- Keep API keys and passwords out of the repo and its history. Use environment variables.

### Be straight in your demo

- Show what works. Say what is mocked.
- Say where your tool gets things wrong. Judges will trust you more for it.

### Who owns what

- Teams own what they build.
- The repo must be public so judges can see it.
- An open license is encouraged, not required. MIT is a good default.
- Respect the terms of any data source or API you use.

### Look after each other

This is a Claude Community event. The
[event terms](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12933905-claude-community-event-terms-and-acknowledgments)
you accepted at registration apply. Be kind, share the floor, and make room for teammates who do
not write code. Their knowledge of the problem is why this works.

## After the awards

One last question for the room: if we combined the best Find, Clear and Fund tools from today,
could one affordable home in Orange County hit $0? Build with that in mind. Clear sources and
clear assumptions are what make tools combinable.
